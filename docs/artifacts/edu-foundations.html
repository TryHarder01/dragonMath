Dino Egg Blaster · Part 1 · Education foundations

Early Math Foundations

What research says about how 4 to 6 year olds learn number, and the rules our dragon and dinosaur game follows as a result.

Kids at this age don't need speed drills. They need to see quantities, say numbers aloud, and move one step at a time along a known path of development.

Why this age

Early math is worth getting right

Strongest predictor

Math skills at school entry predict later achievement better than early reading or attention skills do (Duncan et al., 2007).

Number sense compounds

Kindergarten number sense predicts math achievement through 3rd grade and beyond (Jordan et al., 2009).

Early math can be taught

The research-based Building Blocks preschool curriculum produced large gains in randomized trials (Clements & Sarama, 2008).

Core idea · Clements & Sarama

Learning trajectories

Children's thinking passes through predictable levels. Teach one step past where the child is now, whatever their age. These are the trajectories that matter at 4–6. Each becomes one mini-game.

The five counting principles

A child can know some of these and not others. A child who counts "1, 2, 3, 4" correctly and then counts again when asked "how many?" hasn't grasped cardinality yet. The game checks for it directly.

1

One-to-one

Each object gets exactly one count word.

2

Stable order

The count words always come in the same order.

3 · the big one at 4

Cardinality

The last word said tells how many there are.

4

Abstraction

Anything can be counted: eggs, stomps, roars.

5

Order-irrelevance

Counting in any order gives the same total.

Siegler & Ramani, 2008

A straight number path builds number sense

12345678910
Roll a 3, move from 3 and say "4, 5, 6"

Preschoolers from low-income families played a numbered, linear board game for four 15-minute sessions. Their counting, numeral recognition, magnitude comparison and number-line estimates all improved, and the gains lasted for weeks.

  • A circular board didn't produce the gains.
  • A board with colors instead of numbers didn't either.
  • The key is saying each number aloud while moving.
How to present it · Concrete → Representational → Abstract

Show the quantity, then the symbol

Young children need quantities they can see before a written numeral means anything. At 4–6, every numeral should appear next to a picture of how many it stands for.

Concrete🦕🦕🦕

Real things to tap, move and count.

Representational

Dots, ten-frames, fingers: structured pictures.

Abstract3

The numeral, alongside the picture until the child no longer needs it.

Hirsh-Pasek et al., 2015

What makes an app educational

Active

The child thinks and chooses. Tapping alone doesn't count.

Engaged

Little distraction. Flashy rewards in the middle of a task hurt learning.

Meaningful

It connects to stories and things the child cares about. Dinosaurs qualify.

Social

A parent playing alongside and talking about it helps.

Emotion & practice

No pressure, short sessions

  • Math anxiety appears by 1st–2nd grade and is linked to lower scores. Adults' anxiety spreads to kids (Ramirez et al., 2013; Beilock et al., 2010).
  • Timed drills are a poor fit at this age. The original Math Blaster ran on speed pressure, so this is the one thing we deliberately change.
  • Aim for about 80% success with adaptive difficulty. Too easy bores kids and too hard discourages them.
  • Short daily sessions of 5–15 minutes, with spaced review of easier skills.
  • After a mistake, show the model: count the dots together. Don't just buzz.
  • Praise the strategy: "You counted every egg!"

The NAEYC/NCTM joint position statement, the National Research Council's Mathematics Learning in Early Childhood (2009) and the IES practice guide Teaching Math to Young Children (2013) agree: focus on number, follow developmental progressions, track progress, and do a little math every day. What this means for our game

Ten design rules

  • One mini-game per trajectory, with levels that follow its steps.
  • Adapt per skill: move up after 3 correct in a row, down after 2 misses.
  • Numerals always come with dots, frames or dinos. The lowest levels show only pictures.
  • Speak every prompt. Pre-readers can play without an adult reading for them.
  • No timers, lives or game over. The arcade feel comes from movement and dragons.
  • Mistakes become mini-lessons: highlight and count aloud, then try again.
  • Include a straight 1–10 number path with each hop said aloud.
  • Ten-frames everywhere to build "make 5" and "make 10".
  • Rewards come between rounds. A baby dino hatches after each set, never during a problem.
  • Short sessions: suggest a break after 5–8 minutes, and design for a parent to play along.
